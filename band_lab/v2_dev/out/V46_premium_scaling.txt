============================================================================================
THE LEVERAGE CONSTRAINT — what settles it without a regression
============================================================================================

  SOXL is a 2.98x daily-reset fund on the index SOXX tracks, so its return
  is 2.98x SOXX's every day and its volatility is 2.98x SOXX's at every
  horizon -- realised AND implied. That is mechanical, not empirical.

  Now suppose the premium were ADDITIVE, the same +10.9 points on both:

      IV_SOXX = RV_SOXX - 10.9 = 38.5 - 10.9 = 27.6%
      IV_SOXL = RV_SOXL - 10.9 = 114.5 - 10.9 = 103.6%

  But a 2.98x fund's options must price at 2.98x the index's vol:

      2.98 x IV_SOXX = 2.98 x 27.6 = 82.1%

  Those disagree by 21.5 volatility points -- exactly (lev-1) x 10.9.
  An additive premium on both legs of a levered pair therefore requires the
  market to price SOXL volatility 3.76x the index's while the fund
  delivers 2.98x. That is a 22-point relative-value gap sitting in the two
  most liquid semiconductor chains in the market, permanently.

  **It does not exist. So the premium cannot be additive.** It scales with the
  volatility level, which is what the regression's point estimate said
  (b = 1.13, a = -3.4) but could not establish on 54 independent
  windows. The leverage identity establishes it on arithmetic instead.

  Under the ADDITIVE model V44 assumed, SMH's edge would be +10.9 against a

  above says that model cannot be right.

  SOXX fails under either: +3.7 edge against its own 8.0 spread. Its
  volatility is SMH's but its options cost 2.8x as much to trade.
